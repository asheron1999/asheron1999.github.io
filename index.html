Heritage

Your people train you in one or two skills at no cost. Everyone is also trained in Jump, Run, Magic Defense, and Loyalty for free.

Class

Attributes

Experience targets

Set the values you are aiming for. Cost depends on your starting point, so a skill raised from a high base is far cheaper. Untrained skills can't be raised with experience at all. Direct investment caps at 190 points for an attribute, 208 for a trained skill, and 226 for a specialized one, and the whole plan is held inside a level 126 character's lifetime experience. The two rule sets differ in attribute costs and in specialized skills above the cut-off, where the Sybex guide's launch curve kept climbing and Turbine's December 2000 revision brought it down to roughly a tenth of trained cost. Below the cut-off the two agree exactly.

Where the sources disagree, the game data wins. Skill costs, formulas, descriptions, icons, class templates, attribute spreads and portraits are read from the retail client's own portal.dat (October 1999): the skill table at 0x0E000004 and the character-generation table at 0x0E000002, the character-creation text at 0x31000001 onward, and the starting areas and clothing in CharGen, the vital table at 0x0E000003 (which confirms Health = Endurance ÷ 2, Stamina = Endurance and Mana = Self), and the spell table at 0x0E00000E — 1,628 spells with their school, difficulty, mana cost and description, the portal and lifestone travel spells grouped for convenience, with the travel spells placed in the level tier matching their difficulty rather than the numeral in their name, stored with their text nibble-swapped. Attribute and vital icons are the client's own attribute-panel art (0x060002C4–C9 and 0x06000FA3–FA5), the same nine the game draws beside Strength through Mana. The 330 attribute points, the 50 skill credits and each heritage's free skills are stored per heritage in that same table, so those totals are the client's too, not the manual's. That data is what the game actually ran on, so it supersedes both printed guides wherever they differ — and they do. The books get four class templates and four skill formulas wrong, and drop three skills from the Aluvian warrior. Every one of the client's 21 class and heritage combinations spends exactly 50 skill credits and 330 attribute points, which is the check that exposed those errors.

    
Four eras. Each setting is read from that build's own portal.dat. The three archives differ in format as much as in content: Preview 2 (October 1998) and Beta 0 (April 1999) index their files with 37 branch slots and 24-byte directory entries, retail uses 62 and 12; Preview 2 stores table text in plain bytes with 32-bit lengths, Beta 0 plain with 16-bit lengths, retail nibble-swapped with 16-bit lengths. Dark Majesty (September 2001) returns to retail's directory layout but stores skill text plainly and spell text nibble-swapped. They hold 8,171, 14,098, 17,794 and 30,397 files respectively. All 21 class and heritage combinations balance to exactly 50 credits in every era, which is the check that validates each dataset independently.

    
Dark Majesty renames every class again — Bow Hunter, Swashbuckler, Life Caster, War Mage, Wayfarer, Soldier — drops the Enchanter entirely, leaving seven, and rebuilds every attribute spread around values of 10 and 100. It keeps retail's 35 skills and costs but moves Magic Defense to ÷ 7, and gives each appraisal skill its own icon where retail shared one; those seven new icons are read from its own archive. Its spell table holds 2,287 entries against retail's 1,628, with difficulties raised again: Portal Tie 150, Summon Portal I 200. The Suggested templates section carries 17 player-submitted builds from the Dark Majesty strategy guide (Beth Loubet and Tuesday Frase, Sybex, 2002, ISBN 0782140599); every one spreads exactly 330 attribute points, which is how they were validated, though two — Extreme Dagger and Eighty-Two — list more skills than 50 credits can buy at creation. Dark Majesty grants an automatic skill credit at level 32, counted here for that era only, which takes a level 126 character from 86 credits to 87. The Aun Ralirea toggle in the advancement section adds the quest's further credit from level 35, bringing the total to 88. Neither is read from the archive; level awards and quest rewards live on the server rather than in the client's tables.

    
Beta 0 sits between the other two. It keeps Preview 2's 32 skills, its eight classes (Adventurer, Healer, Rogue) and its six starting towns, but has already taken retail's Dagger formula, Missile Defense divisor, magic-school divisor, Appraise Magic Item divisor and two-skill heritage grants. It still has Preview 2's Magic Defense ÷ 6, Leadership and Loyalty on raw Self, and Healing at 8 to train. Its 1,622 spells carry a different difficulty curve: Portal Tie is 50 rather than 100, Summon Portal I is 70 rather than 150.

    
The 1998 Preview 2 setting is read from that build's own portal.dat, whose directory uses 37 branch slots and 24-byte entries rather than retail's 62 and 12, and whose tables store plain text with 32-bit lengths instead of retail's nibble-swapped 16-bit ones. It has 32 skills rather than 35 — Alchemy, Cooking and Fletching did not exist — eight classes including Adventurer, Healer and Rogue, one free skill per heritage instead of two, six starting towns, and five placeholder spells. Fourteen skills changed between the builds: Magic Defense divided by 6 rather than 10, the magic schools by 6 rather than 4, Missile Defense by 3 rather than 5, Dagger keyed off Strength rather than Quickness, Leadership and Loyalty off raw Self, Healing cost 8 to train, and Loyalty was not yet free. Four skills carry the description “THIS SKILL IS NOT YET IMPLEMENTED.” Skill icons are byte-identical between the two builds, so the same art serves both; class descriptions have no equivalent in the preview and are carried over from retail. All 21 of its class and heritage combinations spend exactly 50 credits, the same check that validates the retail data.

    
The printed guides remain the source for what the client does not carry: the attribute and secondary experience tables, the character level curve, and the heritage and class descriptions — Asheron's Call Official Strategies & Secrets by Rusel DeMaria (Sybex Inc., November 1999, 344 pages, ISBN 0782126545), with the four skill classes described in the boxed game manual.

    
Two things the guides got wrong or left out are settled by the client: Unarmed Combat, absent from table 2.1 altogether, costs 6 to train and 6 to specialize; and the Aluvian warrior, printed 12 credits short, does train Appraise Armor, Appraise Weapon and Assess Creature.

    
Experience is the one area no client covers, in any era — advancement was computed on the server, and searching all four archives and both executables for the known cost sequences finds nothing. It therefore comes from print. The Dark Majesty setting uses that guide's own chapter 3: its attribute and secondary costs are genuinely different (110 to raise an attribute one point from base, against 156 in 1999), while its trained and specialized skill costs match Turbine's December 2000 curves exactly and its level curve is unchanged from 1999. Its attribute table is printed to 170 points above base; the last 20 to the cap are extended along the tail ratio. That table also settles an earlier puzzle: the two unlabelled columns in the December 2000 update, which matched none of the 1999 tables, are these revised attribute and secondary costs. Total XP for a level is round(((level+5)5 − 7,776) ÷ 9), which reproduces all 49 rows of the guide's table 4.2 exactly; its prose claim that level 100 needs 66,254,067 points contradicts that table and is treated as a misprint. Attribute and secondary costs are transcribed from tables 4.3 and 4.4 — every row checked against a running-sum and a curve-smoothness test, with damaged rows read back off the page scans.

    
Trained and specialized costs come from Turbine's official guide update of 5 December 2000, which revises the specialization figures published in chapter 4 and runs to the caps: 208 points in a trained skill costs 4,203,819,496 XP and 226 in a specialized one costs 4,100,490,438, both just inside a level 126 character's lifetime. The 1999 setting restores the original curve as Sybex printed it, to 186 points; beyond that the table stops, so those rows are extended along its tail ratio.
